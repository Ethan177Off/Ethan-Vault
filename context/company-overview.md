## Business Overview

The business focuses on developing the Taiwan market for CHIBA gloves, with an initial emphasis on cycling and fitness products.

CHIBA is a German glove brand.

The current business model centers on:

- Brand representation / distribution
- B2B channel development
- Retail partnerships
- Product positioning
- Sales enablement
- Market development

The objective is not merely to sell individual products.

The broader objective is to establish a repeatable and scalable distribution system in Taiwan.

## Commercial Strategy

The emerging commercial model combines:

### Product Selection

Identify products that fit the actual purchasing behavior and price structure of each channel.

### Positioning

Avoid treating all products as equivalent.

Build clear differentiation between entry, core, and premium options.

### Channel Enablement

Retail performance may depend on making the product easier for sales staff to explain.

Useful tools may include:

- Product comparison
- Good-Better-Best structure
- Hero SKU strategy
- POP materials
- Sales talking points
- Product-benefit cards

### Controlled Expansion

Prefer validating channel-product fit before committing excessive inventory or resources.

Use:

Hypothesis
→ Pilot
→ Measure
→ Learn
→ Adjust
→ Scale

## Pricing Philosophy

Pricing should not be determined only from cost-plus calculations.

Important variables include:

- Consumer willingness to pay

- Retailer margin
- Distributor economics
- Competitive alternatives
- Brand positioning
- SKU role

- Promotional flexibility

ATLAS should evaluate pricing as part of the full channel economics.

## Channel Partnership Philosophy

A good partner should provide more than purchase volume.

Evaluate potential partners based on:

- Market access
- Customer fit
- Sales capability
- Brand presentation
- Operational reliability
- Reorder potential
- Commercial terms
- Strategic alignment

Possible commercial structures may include:

- Wholesale purchase
- Consignment

- Limited initial assortment

The appropriate structure should depend on partner quality and downside risk.

## Brand Development Principle

The objective is to build durable market presence rather than rely entirely on one-time transactions.

Important brand-building elements include:

- Consistent positioning
- Clear product hierarchy
- Hero products
- Retail education
- Repeatable sales language
- Customer feedback
- Channel-specific merchandising
- Long-term partner relationships

Brand development and sales execution should reinforce each other.

---

## Operating Principle

Do not scale complexity faster than validated demand.

Prefer:

1. Identify opportunity
2. Test with limited exposure
3. Collect evidence
4. Improve the model
5. Increase commitment only when justified

For reversible commercial decisions, bias toward experimentation.

For large inventory commitments, exclusivity, long-term contracts, or material financial exposure, increase diligence.

## Key Business Questions

ATLAS should continuously help evaluate:

1. Which channel has the highest expected return on effort?
2. Which products should function as Hero SKUs?
3. What price architecture best supports the channel?
4. What prevents retail staff from recommending the product?
5. What customer pain point does each SKU actually solve?
6. Which partnerships are worth deeper investment?
7. What assumptions about the market remain unverified?
8. What should be tested before scaling?
9. Where is capital being tied up unnecessarily?
10. What creates a defensible long-term position?
