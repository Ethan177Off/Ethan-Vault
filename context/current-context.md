# Current Context — CHIBA Taiwan

> Last updated: 2026-09-01
> Purpose: Current operating snapshot for ATLAS.

## Current Stage

The CHIBA Taiwan business is currently in early channel-development and market-validation stage.

The immediate objective is not nationwide scale.

The current objective is to validate:

- Which retail partners are worth developing
- Which products fit the Taiwan market
- Which price bands convert
- Which sales messages work
- Which partnership structure produces repeatable sales
- How much inventory commitment is justified

The business should prioritize learning and repeatability before aggressive expansion.

---

## Current Commercial Situation

Outbound business development has begun producing responses.

Multiple potential partners have replied, and at least one relationship has progressed toward more formal cooperation discussions.

This indicates that the current market-development approach is beginning to generate traction, but partner-product-channel fit is not yet fully validated.

ATLAS should not interpret early partner interest as proof of scalable demand.

The next stage is conversion from:

Interest
→ Pilot
→ Sell-through
→ Reorder
→ Repeatable channel model

---

## Key Market Signal

Recent retailer feedback suggests that the Taiwan cycling glove market is fragmented.

There is no single glove brand clearly dominating the entire category.

Many cycling-related brands and retailers offer gloves as part of a broader product assortment.

This creates both an opportunity and a challenge:

### Opportunity

CHIBA may be able to build a differentiated position without displacing one overwhelmingly dominant incumbent.

### Challenge

Retailers and consumers already have many acceptable alternatives, so product quality alone may not create enough pull.

The business needs clear reasons for:

- Retailers to stock CHIBA
- Staff to recommend CHIBA
- Consumers to choose CHIBA
- Partners to reorder CHIBA

---

## Price Signal

Retail feedback indicates that approximately NT$590 is an important existing sales band for cycling gloves in at least one relevant retail environment.

This should be treated as a market signal, not a universal market truth.

ATLAS should evaluate:

- Which CHIBA SKU can compete effectively near this band
- Whether margin economics remain attractive
- Whether premium SKUs need stronger differentiation
- Whether entry / core / premium architecture improves conversion

Potential commercial structure:

Good
→ Accessible entry

Better
→ Core volume product

Best
→ Premium / specialized product

Do not force every SKU into the same price logic.

## Retail Execution Problem

One important commercial observation is that glove sales may not receive strong proactive promotion from retail staff.

This means the business cannot rely only on:

> "The product is good, so it will sell."

CHIBA needs to reduce the effort required for retail staff to understand and recommend the product.

Potential tools include:

- Hero SKU focus
- Good-Better-Best comparison
- Simple product-benefit cards
- POP materials
- Staff talking points
- Clear pain-point communication
- Product comparison sheets

The objective is to make the selling logic obvious.

---

## Current Value Proposition Direction

Product communication should translate technical features into customer outcomes.

Current promising customer-facing themes include:

- Reduced hand discomfort
- Reduced numbness / pressure
- Vibration management
- Better long-ride comfort
- Grip
- Protection
- Weather protection

Avoid communicating only technical construction details.

The commercial question is:

> Why should the customer care?

Every Hero SKU should have a clear answer.

## Current Strategic Hypothesis

The current working hypothesis is:

CHIBA can gain traction in Taiwan by combining:

1. Strong product quality
2. Clear Hero SKUs
3. Relevant mainstream price points
4. Retailer-friendly sales tools
5. Good-Better-Best product architecture
6. Controlled pilot partnerships
7. Fast replenishment and low partner friction

This remains a hypothesis.

It must be validated through actual sell-through and reorder data.

## Current Highest-Leverage Question

The most important near-term business question is:

> Can we convert retailer interest into repeatable sell-through and reorder behavior?

This is more important than maximizing the number of stores contacted.

A partner that repeatedly sells and reorders is more valuable than many partners that only express interest.

## Near-Term Priorities

### Priority 1 — Convert Active Partner Discussions

Move the strongest current opportunities toward a concrete pilot or commercial structure.

### Priority 2 — Define Hero SKUs

Limit complexity.

Select a small number of products with clear roles, customer pain points, and price positions.

### Priority 3 — Prepare Retail Sales Tools

Build simple materials that help staff answer:

- Who is this product for?
- What problem does it solve?
- Why is it better?
- Why does it cost this price?
- Which model should I recommend?

### Priority 4 — Define Pilot Economics

Before committing inventory, understand:

- Purchase cost
- Selling price
- Retail margin
- Gross margin
- Inventory exposure
- Expected reorder cycle

### Priority 5 — Establish Learning Metrics

Track more than initial orders.

Important signals include:

- Sell-through
- Reorder rate
- SKU-level performance
- Retail staff feedback
- Consumer feedback
- Price resistance
- Inventory turnover
