# CHIBA Taiwan Channel Development — Status

> Last updated: 2026-09-01
> Status: Active
> Stage: Early channel validation
> Owner: Ethan
> Strategic support: ATLAS

## Objective

Build a repeatable and commercially viable Taiwan distribution and retail channel model for CHIBA gloves.

The immediate objective is not maximum store count.

The immediate objective is to prove:

> Partner interest → Pilot → Sell-through → Reorder

A repeatable reorder cycle is the primary evidence that channel-product fit is becoming real.

---

## Current Stage

The project has moved beyond pure cold outreach.

Several potential business contacts have responded, and at least one retailer relationship has progressed into substantive cooperation discussions.

The current focus is converting interest into concrete commercial pilots.

Do not interpret meetings, positive feedback, or initial interest as validated demand.

---

## Primary Active Partner

### 路達

Current relationship:

Active business-development opportunity.

Key market feedback obtained:

- Cycling gloves are a fragmented category in Taiwan.
- No single glove brand appears to dominate the entire market.
- Retail sales data suggests a meaningful consumer price band around NT$590 in this retail environment.
- Road cycling gloves are an important existing product category.
- Retail staff may not proactively promote gloves without simple selling logic.

Implication:

CHIBA needs to make product selection and recommendation easy for the retailer, not merely provide a good product.

## Current Commercial Hypothesis

CHIBA Taiwan can gain traction by combining:

1. Focused Hero SKUs
2. Clear consumer pain-point communication
3. Good-Better-Best product architecture
4. Relevant mainstream pricing
5. Retailer-friendly POP and sales tools
6. Controlled partner pilots
7. Efficient replenishment

This is a working hypothesis.

It must be validated by real sales behavior.

## Candidate Hero SKU Roles

### Core / Mainstream Road Cycling Glove

Role:

Primary volume candidate.

Key validation questions:

- Can it compete around the mainstream purchasing band?
- Is retailer margin acceptable?
- Is the consumer benefit easy to explain?
- Does it generate reorder?

### Winter / Wind Protection

Role:

Seasonal differentiated product.

Potential advantage:

Clearer functional reason to purchase and potentially stronger premium positioning.

### Women's Short-Finger Glove

Role:

Segment-specific differentiated product.

Needs validation for:

- Actual demand
- Fit
- merchandising
- price acceptance

No Hero SKU should be treated as permanently selected until supported by sales evidence.

## Retail Sales Enablement

Current sales-support concepts include:

- Good-Better-Best
- Hero SKU strategy
- POP pain-point communication
- Product benefit cards
- Simple staff talking points
- Customer use-case explanation

Desired staff experience:

> Customer describes need → Staff quickly identifies appropriate CHIBA glove → Staff can explain why.

The sales system should reduce cognitive load for both staff and customer.

---

## Commercial Terms to Resolve

For each serious partner, evaluate:

- MOQ
- Initial order quantity
- Wholesale vs consignment
- Retail margin
- Gross margin
- Payment terms
- Replenishment
- Inventory ownership
- Return policy
- Marketing support
- Online channel treatment
- Territory expectations

Avoid accepting structurally weak terms simply to secure an early account.

---

## Validation Metrics

Do not measure success only by number of partners contacted.

Priority metrics:

- Initial order conversion
- Sell-through by SKU
- Reorder rate
- Time to reorder
- Inventory turnover
- Retailer feedback
- Staff recommendation behavior
- Consumer price resistance
- Consumer product feedback

---

## Current Risks

### Risk 1 — Premature SKU Expansion

Too many products can dilute inventory, training, and messaging.

### Risk 2 — Mistaking Interest for Demand

A positive meeting is not equivalent to sell-through.

### Risk 3 — Weak Retailer Economics

A good consumer product may still fail if the partner margin or inventory model is unattractive.

### Risk 4 — Product Complexity

If staff cannot explain the differences quickly, recommendation probability falls.

### Risk 5 — Capital Locked in Inventory

Inventory commitments should increase only after stronger evidence of sell-through.

---

## Highest-Leverage Question

> Which combination of partner + Hero SKU + price + sales message produces the first repeatable reorder cycle?

Until this is answered, broad expansion is secondary.
